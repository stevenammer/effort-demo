# Browser voice capture and local safety note

Voice capture is off until the person chooses **Use browser voice capture** in Capture. It uses only the browser-provided `SpeechRecognition` or `webkitSpeechRecognition` capability, when available. The transcript is placed in the ordinary Capture textarea as editable draft text. It is never saved as an original until the person uses **Save original**. The app does not record or retain audio.

Speech recognition is implemented by the browser, not E.F.F.O.R.T. Some browser vendors send microphone audio to their own speech service. E.F.F.O.R.T. cannot control that, does not make a request itself, and says this before listening. A browser without the capability shows an unavailable state rather than pretending it worked.

The safety note is a small deterministic local keyword check applied to Capture text and to a helper-review question before it is kept. It recognizes a few direct first-person statements about suicide, self-harm, or not being able to stay safe, including phrases such as “kill myself,” “end my life,” “take my own life,” “want to die,” and “don't want to be alive.” It deliberately requires first-person phrasing for those phrases, which avoids ordinary third-person references but cannot reliably distinguish a quoted fictional first-person line from a personal statement. It never diagnoses, alerts anyone, blocks, deletes, changes, or transmits writing. A person can dismiss the note and still save a capture or keep a helper request.

This check is intentionally limited. It can miss indirect, unfamiliar, misspelled, or contextual language, and it can show a false positive for text that isn't an immediate safety concern. It is not a risk assessment or a substitute for human support. When it appears, it offers the US Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, call or text 988. Outside the US, use local emergency or crisis support.
